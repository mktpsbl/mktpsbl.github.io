---
title: "8 Months with the MacBook Neo: I Absolutely Love It—Just Don’t Buy It Right Now"
date: 2026-11-14
---

I pre-ordered the Macbook Neo the same day that it was announced, which I feel very lucky since it got backordered pretty quick after it was released initially. I’ve been using it as my main and only laptop for the past 8 months, and in general I honestly really really like it. (do note that I bought it before the price hikes)

I think it’s important I give a bit of context of what I do on my laptop, so my thoughts can be accurate as the kind of user that I am. I will be completely honest, I am definitely considered a light user on the spectrum of light use to pro use. I pretty much live in the web browser, with google workspace apps as my most heavily used webapps. And that’s about it, that’s the most power I will push in a laptop, at most many tabs and browser windows opened at a time. In other words, this article is not a review of the Macbook Neo, but simply my thoughts and opinions about my experience with the Macbook Neo. 

Starting off with why I even bought the Macbook Neo so decisively, on launch day. Before the Macbook Neo was even released, I was already looking for my next laptop or what I usually prefer, a detachable. Before the Neo, my setup was Galaxy tab s9+ with the keyboard cover on the go, and a windows desktop PC when I’m at home. I started to be interested in a new machine when Samsung decided to pull the classic dex from their galaxy tabs, which is about 5 months before the Neo. In the meantime, I have been searching for my next machine, which spanned from Surface Pro to various chromeOS devices. Here are the reasons I didn’t decide on any before the Neo. I don’t like Windows, straight up; to me Windows is not a user-friendly system with too many legacy elements. I know that appeals to a lot and I am not trying to offend Windows users, but for me and the light user that I am, I just prefer a prettier, user-friendly OS/UI. Other than Windows being a dealbreaker, another one is x86/x64 chips. ARM has proven to me a lot more efficient than x86/x64, and that’s very important for me, whether that’s longer battery life or less heating. Again, I am just a light user, so I don’t need my machine to be able to run professional, legacy programs that would be more fit on x86/x64. With these two being dealbreakers for me, I am left with little choices, mostly Chromebooks. However, Chromebooks are mostly low-end (even though I am a light user I still appreciate premium builds) and I know that Googlebooks are coming out later this year (which I have been very excited for and was my plan for an upgrade) so buying a ChromeOS device just did not make sense with me. The reason why I didn’t consider Macbooks is because like I said I am a light user and to me every Macbook at the time was on the higher end, which I just will never need that power. 

Now that I’ve illustrated why I didn’t settle with a device before the Neo is released, let’s go into what Neo offered that made me pre-order almost immediately. If you consider the reasons why I didn’t settle with a device, then you should be able to make out my wants in my new device. It was an efficient arm chip, premium build, and a pretty, user-friendly UI. That is exactly what Macbooks offer, and the only reason I didn’t buy a Macbook before this was because there wasn’t a Macbook meant for a light, entry-level user like me. What’s interesting is, I also pre-ordered after watching the introduction video/ad for the Neo (Yes the ad got me!). The introduction for Macbook Neo I think is one of my favorite tech ads of all time, sending the exact message that makes the Macbook Neo stand out. The ad basically showed the Macbook neo being constructed in a very “apple way,” while showing the laptop doing basic tasks like watching movies, texting, browning, or light photo editing. That is the exact right message for the Neo: “A Macbook now at a budget price, while preserving all the elements of what makes Macbooks premium.” To me, with my use cases of a laptop, that’s basically telling me that I will now be able to use Macbooks, what was only a higher-end device, at a lower price without the sacrifices of a lower price. Now, going into some more specific reasons and complete personal preferences: symmetrical bezels, light-coloured keys, and a fun colour. The symmetrical bezels really attracted me as almost no other laptop maker, even with the most high-end Windows devices, have symmetrical bezels; they always keep the sides thin while using a thicker top to be able to fit in the webcam. While that seems more premium to others, I’ve always appreciated the symmetrical sides of Macbook more than a thin bezel. Macbook Neo is also the first Macbook to have light-coloured keys in almost 15 years. I personally just prefer my tech to be lighter-coloured. Lastly, colours! I’ve always believed that if a product is positioned as a budget product, then it shouldn’t try to be premium (for example, plastic trying to look like glass), rather it should shine in its own way. The iPad follows the same concept where the standard iPad has fun colours while the further up you go, the more subtle the tone is to establish a premium feeling. I may have spent too much time talking about why I made the purchase, but that is genuinely when I started to love the product; I just loved the message of the product and knew that it’s gonna be up to my standards before I even got my hands on it. 

Let’s finally dive into the actual experience of the Macbook Neo for me in the past 8 months. I’m going to just start by saying I love it, I absolutely love it. This does not mean that I think all of you reading this should buy it or that any other laptop in this price range that isn’t a Macbook Neo is not as good (especially with the recent price hikes). My point is that for me and my wants in a laptop, this has been a very pleasant experience. There are so many things that I love about this laptop, but if I have to choose one aspect as my favourite, that would undoubtedly be the build quality. I have been using this laptop for 8 months, but everytime I boot it up, I still say the same thing to myself, “this feels so premium.” From even just the aluminium unibody and passing the one-finger opening test makes me feel that this laptop belongs in a higher price segment. The experience is exactly what I think the advertisement advertises, the same premium experience of a Macbook at a lower price. While yes, I am aware that a Macbook Neo compared to a Macbook Air still lacks in many ways, I think Apple did a good job at sacrificing the right things to still produce a laptop at the standard of a Macbook. The sacrifices include backlit keys, haptic trackpad, weight, battery size, bezel thickness, and a lack of touch id (on my model). These are very real shortcomings that for many users are dealbreakers. But for me, Below is completely my opinion, but it may reflect what other users feel about it as well. For me, I don’t work in the dark, it hurts my eyes, so backlit keys are negligible for me. In general I also just prefer non-backlight keycaps as the legends do not have to be translucent, which in my opinion looks better as it’s more contrasty. The trackpad on the Macbook Neo is another highlight of this laptop. The media was pretty surprised when it was revealed that Macbook Neo does not have a haptic trackpad. However, the trackpad on the Macbook Neo isn’t just a generic diving board trackpad, it is a glass trackpad that you can still click with equal force on any corner of the trackpad. I have a friend who owns an M1 Pro Macbook Pro who also says that the Neo trackpad feels basically as premium as other Macbook haptic trackpads. The thickness of the bezels on the Macbook Neo is noticeably thicker than the Macbook Air and Macbook Pro, but to me I don’t mind it at all. In fact I even prefer it over the designs on the other Macbooks. Aforementioned, I care more about symmetrical bezels rather than the absolute thinness of it, which is exactly the preference apple went with here. Rather than using a thicker top like any other laptop, even the most-high end ones, Apple just decided to thicken the side bezels to create a symmetrical look, similar to the iPad. Now, lastly, a sacrifice, the lack of touch ID. I already know I am pretty much alone on this one but I just don’t mind it at all. Touch ID on Apple devices has always been on the slower side, and the fact that it’s positioned on the top right means I have to reach my right hand up for a second or two and then reach back down. I always find myself to operate quicker by just typing the password and then moving on, without having to take my right hand off the center of the keyboard at all. 

At this point, I think this article has basically become an ad for the Macbook Neo, so I think I must talk about the things I don’t like about the device and why just because I like it doesn’t mean that it is the best in its price segment. First, 8gb of RAM. While yes, I have been emphasizing that I am only a light user and this device fits the type of user I am, I just feel like 8gb ram in a laptop in 2026 is still just too low. While I have not run into any situation where I would need more ram for my tasks, a device with less ram just won’t last as long as one that has 12gb ram. This imperfection is especially highlighted considering that the next Macbook Neo will most likely have 12gb ram and is going to release early next year. This just makes me feel that I missed out by buying the first generation. For that reason I actually don’t recommend the Macbook Neo right now, no matter the kind of user you are, just wait for a few months for the 12gb ram. Secondly, this one is a little bit tricky to put it as something I don’t like about it. That is the battery, and that is not because the device has subpar battery life. The battery life on this device has been brilliant, and better than what I used previously as my laptop, a Galaxy Tab S9+. The reason why I put it as something I don’t like about it is because it has a considerably smaller battery size than the Macbook Air while weighing heavier. The reason that it weighs heavier is because on the right and left side of the trackpad, rather than filling that space with batteries, that space is filled with empty weights. I have a few theories why Apple decided to do this. First of all, it is to preserve passing the one-hand opening test. If that space is filled with batteries instead of empty weight, then it would be too top-heavy that opening the laptop requires two hands, which makes the laptop not feel as premium. Another theory that I have is product segmentation. Apple has always been adamant about making sure their more expensive products are better than the more budget line, even if that means deliberately sabotaging the lower-priced product. We see that with non-pro iPads being restricted to 60hz even to this day, or only allowing usb-c 2.0 transfer speeds on non-pro iPhones. If the Macbook Neo used batteries instead of empty weights left and right of the trackpad, it would beat the Macbook Air in two different ways. It would have a better battery life (most-likely with the better efficiency) and a lighter weight. Especially the lighter weight, that is what the Macbook Air is named after. 

Here are some reasons why I actually don’t recommend the Macbook Neo right now. First, the price hikes. I pre-ordered mine, which meant I got mine before the price hikes, which is a 100$ discount from what it is right now. With the price hike, I am not sure if Macbook Neo is still the best deal out there like it was when it was first released. Second, the improved competition. Since Macbook Neo has been released, other manufacturers have quickly made efforts to price-match the Macbook Neo with similar or better offerings. Some examples of this include the Dell XPS or the new HP Omnibook 5; whether it is battery or price-to-performance ratio, they actually have a clear advantage over the Macbook Neo (but me personally I still wouldn’t consider them because Windows). Third and I think the biggest, the next Macbook Neo. The next Macbook Neo is rumored to come out in just a few months and it will have a major upgrade, ram. To be able to get 12gb ram with the same price as it is priced right now can re-establish Macbook Neo as the value king. Even though light-users don’t use much ram, more ram means more longevity for the device. 

To conclude, I love my Macbook Neo, there is no question about that. I love everything it stands for, and the actual experience of using it for the past 8 months has not disappointed one bit. However, I wouldn’t recommend one right now unless you absolutely need a new laptop right now.
